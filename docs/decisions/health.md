# lil health

<!-- Part of docs/DECISIONS.md, the index of decisions (851-2791). -->

- **What it is:** Apple Health on the Mac. macOS has no Health app (as of macOS 27).
  - The iOS app reads Apple Health and uploads it to `api.lil.tech`.
  - The macOS app syncs it and renders it like iPad Health.
- **Why a server:** App Store guideline 5.1.3 forbids storing personal health data in iCloud, so the iPhone can't hand data to the Mac through CloudKit.
- **iOS app:** sign in, consent, grant Health access, then **Summary**, the account's cards computed from the iPhone's HealthKit (851-3211, `cards.md`), with the upload's status (Sync Status) a row or toolbar button there. It syncs the account's cards, and nothing else (851-3205): Health data only goes up.
- **Cards** (851-3192, being built; designed in [`cards.md`](cards.md), 851-3193): Summary's metrics become cards people and agents create, a chart each from a versioned card spec, synced across devices. The Mac computes them from its SQLite and the iPhone from HealthKit, which makes the iPhone more than a bridge. Statements here that cards change are listed in `cards.md` (What changes elsewhere), and each ticket updates its own.
- **Data:** v1 syncs every readable HealthKit type, including workouts and routes, ECG, heartbeat series, audiograms, medications and State of Mind.
  - Clinical records (Health Records) are out of scope, because they need an entitlement that depends on App Review approval. Health Documents (CDA) are a different type, which the standard HealthKit entitlement reads, so they sync (below).
  - Workouts are `health_workout` records: the overall activity type, times and duration, plus their activities (multisport legs), events (pauses, laps, segments) and per-type statistics as JSON, in the catalog's units. The iPhone reads them through the same anchored backfill as samples. Routes come separately (851-2566).
  - State of Mind logs (`health_state_of_mind`) and GAD-7/PHQ-9 assessments (`health_scored_assessment`) keep HealthKit's raw values for kinds, labels, associations, risks and answers, so the Mac can name them in the user's language.
  - Audiograms (`health_audiogram`) keep each tested frequency with its per-ear tests (sensitivity and clamping range in dBHL, conduction type, masking), as HealthKit's iOS 18.1 model has them.
  - ECGs (`health_electrocardiogram`) keep their classification, symptoms status, average heart rate and sampling frequency in the row. The voltages (lead I) are a blob of little-endian 32-bit floats in microvolts, one per measurement, about 60 KB for a 30-second recording. The iPhone reads them 50 ECGs to a page, so a page's blobs stay small in memory. The symptoms themselves are category samples, synced like any other, and linked to their ECG (851-4153, below).
  - Workout routes (`health_workout_route`) keep their locations in a blob, 44 bytes each (time, latitude and longitude as 64-bit floats; altitude, accuracies, speed and course as 32-bit), about 160 KB for an hour at 1 Hz. The iPhone reads them 10 to a page. HealthKit only queries routes by workout, so the iPhone finds each route's workout among the workouts its time overlaps and stores it (`workout`, or `null` if none links it). A later blob format would add a `locationsFormat` field; rows without one are this format. They're precise location data, which the privacy policy says.
  - Heartbeat series (`health_heartbeat_series`) keep each beat's time (seconds since the start) and the indices of beats preceded by a gap, as JSON in the row. They're about a minute and a hundred beats each (~2 KB), so a blob per reading would cost an R2 object, a quota row and a download, thousands of times a year, for little. The iPhone reads them 200 to a page, since each needs its own query.
  - **Quantity series** (`health_quantity_series`, 851-4149, from the sync audit of 2026-11-09): a quantity sample can hold many values, its `count`, as Apple's workout builders and apps using `HKQuantitySeriesSampleBuilder` write them (a heart rate every few seconds, steps a minute at a time). HealthKit hands one back as one sample, `HKDiscreteQuantitySample` or `HKCumulativeQuantitySample`, whose `quantity` is the whole series': its sum, or its average, time-weighted for heart rate (measured in the iOS 27.2 simulator: 120 values whose plain mean is 119.5 averaged 118.375). The iPhone used to upload only that, so a series counted as one long reading. Now a sample whose `count` is over 1 also goes up as a `health_quantity_series` record under its ID: `count`, then each value's start and duration in seconds since the sample's start (to the millisecond) and its value in the type's unit, as three arrays (`times`, `durations`, `values`), and a discrete series' `minimum`, `maximum` and `mostRecent` with the latter's start and end. The sample's own row doesn't change, so every reader of `health_sample` still works.
    - **A record of its own,** not new `health_sample` fields: a Mac only gets a field of an entity it already keeps by bootstrapping its whole Health copy again (a `syncSchema` bump), while a new entity is caught up by itself (851-3222). Old Macs skip it. **Deleting a sample deletes its series** on the server (`deletedWith: "health_sample"`, `applyMutations`), which logs the series' delete too, so the Macs drop it; the iPhone sends only the sample's, as before, and so does any other path that deletes samples. That needs the series keyed by ID alone, unlike `health_sample`.
    - **Read in small queries, several at once** (851-4182): a page's series' values come from `HKQuantitySeriesSampleQueryDescriptor`s of up to 500 series or 5,000 of their values, by their UUIDs, 8 at once (`HealthKitCardEngine.seriesConcurrency`, as the cards' iPhone engine reads them); one of several series has each value's sample, to tell whose it is, and one of a series alone (a long one) doesn't. It was one query at a time of up to 500 series or 100,000 values: 851-4172 saw 183 IDs take 125 s, which 851-4182 couldn't make happen again (0.3–0.5 s, however the query was made), but the small queries read a year of daily workouts' values in 0.32–0.34 s against 0.90–0.98 s, and 5,000 series of 4 values in 1.1–1.4 s against 1.7–4.1 s, where a query per series took 3.2–3.4 s, each query costing a few milliseconds however few its values (`docs/BENCHMARK.md` › The iPhone's upload). The values are a small part of a page's read, so a whole read takes about what it did. A query that fails, or leaves a series without values (changed or deleted meanwhile), reads the page again, then holds its query's series back, as heartbeat series' do (851-4151). A page holds at most 500,000 series values (about 50 MB as they're encoded): one whose samples' series have more is read again with fewer samples. A series of more than 20,000 values (`HealthEncoder.maxSeriesValues`, at most about 34 bytes of JSON each, so its line stays under 1 MB) goes up with its count and summary only, `times`, `durations` and `values` null, and counts as one reading, as before. Values and the summary are rounded to 12 significant digits, which drops the noise of a conversion to the catalog's unit (`125.00000000000001` count/min for 125): a one-hour workout's heart rate every 5 s is about 8 KB of JSON (2.8 KB gzipped). A series' record doesn't count as a record uploaded; its sample does. One whose series alone didn't go up (refused, or its batch failed or its values couldn't be read when read again) is held back and its sample uncounted once, since sending it again sends and counts the sample too.
    - **Series already uploaded are read again once** (`HealthBackfillState.quantitySeriesEncodings`, `HealthEncoder.quantitySeriesEncoding`): in the first run of everything after the update, once a quantity type has caught up from its anchor, an anchored query of only its series (`HKPredicateKeyPathCount` over 1) reads them all and sends only their `health_quantity_series` records, which don't count as records uploaded, a page at a time, resuming where it stopped. Pages go up as held-back records sent again do (at the type's anchor; a failed batch holds its series back by ID), without the series of records held back, which go up whole when they're tried again. A type read from the start (its first read, Upload Everything Again) reads every series with its values, so needs none.
    - **How common series are** isn't known yet: the simulator only has what its tests write. Counting `count > 1` samples per type on a developer's own iPhone, without uploading anything, would say (851-4173): the Debug-only Diagnostics screen's Quantity Series does (below, Privacy).
    - **Statistics read a series' values** (851-4171; the cards in all three engines since 851-4172, by the same rules, `docs/decisions/cards.md` › Details 851-4172 settled): the Mac's `HealthDatabase.discreteStatistics` (Metric Detail's charts and averages, Browse, highlights) and the tools' `metric` on both servers read each value as a reading of its own, at its own time (`quantityReadings`, in `HealthDatabase/Queries/QuantitySeriesReadings.swift` and the server's `tools/HealthQueries.ts`, shared with the cards). So an arithmetic average counts each value, time weighting runs from value to value (the series' last value until its source's next reading, up to an hour, as any reading), minimums and maximums are the values', and a series split by an hour or a day counts each value where it falls, as HealthKit's statistics split one. The sample's own value, HealthKit's for the whole series, isn't a reading. A series without its values (over 20,000) stays one reading at its start, with its own `minimum` and `maximum` as its extremes. Readings that start together keep single samples first, then series by start, ID and value.
      - **A series' values count for a week after its start, and not past its end** (`seriesLookback`): a range reads the series that started up to a week before it (through a `(type, startDate)` index on the series, Mac migration "Index quantity series by type and start", store migration 30) and ended in it, so a workout crossing midnight counts its values after midnight on the next day. Without a bound, a range would need every older series to know which reach into it; with one, what a range reads doesn't depend on where it starts, so the Mac's per-bucket queries and the server's single pass agree. 20,000 values every 30 s is a week.
      - **The latest reading of a series** (`latestReading`) is its `mostRecent`, with its start and end: `summary`'s latest heart rate and Browse's latest value. HealthKit's average of a workout isn't a reading anyone took. A cumulative series has no `mostRecent`, so its latest is the sample's total, as before.
      - **`samples` lists a series as one sample with `series`**: its `count`, and a discrete series' `minimum`, `maximum`, `mostRecent`, `mostRecentStart` and `mostRecentEnd`. With `seriesValues: true`, each series whose values were uploaded is its values instead, newest first, in its sample's place, each an item with the sample's ID, source and details, its own start, end and value, and `series` saying `count` and `index` (from 0, the earliest). The limit counts items, so a page can end inside a series: its cursor is then `<start>_<id>_<index>`, the last value listed, and the next page goes on from the value before it. A page ending on a series' first value has the sample's cursor, so `_0` isn't one. Values rather than readings because an agent asking for samples wants what was recorded; `metric` is for statistics. The default stays one sample a series, since a workout's values are hundreds of items.
  - **Links** (`health_workout_samples`, `health_workout_effort`, `health_electrocardiogram_symptoms`, 851-4153, from the sync audit of 2026-11-09): HealthKit links samples to a workout and symptoms to an ECG, but gives the links only through queries of their own, which the iPhone didn't make, so the Mac guessed a workout's heart rate from its time and the source with the most readings, and an ECG's page said only "Symptoms Logged" (until 851-4186, below). Measured in the iOS 27.2 simulator with synthetic data (`HealthKitLinksTests`):
    - **A workout's samples** are those its app added to it (`HKWorkoutBuilder.addSamples`, the Watch's heart rate, energy and distance), which `HKQuery.predicateForObjects(from:)` finds, a predicate per type: not another sample recorded at the same time. A query costs about 6 ms a workout for one type and 0.8 ms more per type, so the iPhone asks only for the types of the workout's statistics (`allStatistics`, its and its activities'), which HealthKit computes from the samples added and which named exactly the types added, plus heart rate, 8 workouts at once, each with its page. They go up beside the workout, under its ID: `count`, and `samples`, each type's IDs in order of start, or `null` past 20,000 (`HealthEncoder.maxLinkedSamples`, so a line stays under 1 MB: a day's hike, with energy and distance every few seconds). Every workout gets one, empty if nothing was added, so a Mac knows the difference between none and not read.
    - **An ECG's symptoms** are the symptom category samples logged with it (`HKQuery.predicateForObjectsAssociated(electrocardiogram:)`), asked for of every symptom type the catalog has, only when its `symptomsStatus` says some were logged, and go up beside it the same way. Apps can't create ECGs, so no test reads one from HealthKit; the package's tests cover the encoding.
    - **A workout's effort scores** (`HKWorkoutEffortRelationship`: `WorkoutEffortScore` samples a person rates, `EstimatedWorkoutEffortScore` the Watch's) are related to it, or to a leg of a multisport workout, usually after it's saved, and needn't fall within its times (a score dated an hour after its workout relates fine: the audit's unclear item 4), so its time can't stand in. Relating one doesn't change the workout's anchor, and the relationships' own anchored query (`HKWorkoutEffortRelationshipQuery`) reports one related since but not one unrelated or deleted, so the iPhone reads them whole: every workout's in a run of everything (100 workouts' in 0.18 s), deleting the record of one left without any, and the last week's workouts' in runs for changes to workouts or effort scores, which is when people rate them, so a long history isn't read on every workout. Only the workouts whose scores changed go up (a hash each in a backfill file, `workout-efforts.json`), and once a week a run of everything sends them all again (`effortsResentAt`), since a batch of them the server failed or refused isn't checked (only the types' pages are, 851-4151). A record has the workout's `samples` and `mostRelevant`, HealthKit's own pick among them (`.mostRelevant`: the last related, it seems, not the latest or highest), and `legs`, those of a multisport workout's legs with scores of their own (`activity` being the leg's ID in the workout's `activities`; not named `activities`, which the metadata cleanup scrubs in every entity). Upload Everything Again sends them all again, should a batch of them have failed, and signing out forgets the file.
    - **Records of their own,** under the workout's or ECG's ID, deleted with it (`deletedWith`), not fields of `health_workout` or `health_electrocardiogram`, which a Mac only gets for records it already has by bootstrapping again: a new entity is caught up by itself (851-3222). Old Macs skip them. The samples linked are ordinary `health_sample` records, which may arrive later or be gone.
    - **Workouts and ECGs already uploaded have only their links read again,** once, the way series are (851-4149): `HealthBackfillType.dependentsEncoding` is `HealthEncoder.linksEncoding` for workouts and ECGs, and the first run of everything after the update reads them with an anchored query of their own and sends only the links' records, so neither the workouts nor the ECGs' voltages go up again. Links count as part of their record, not as records uploaded, and one the server refuses holds its workout or ECG back to go up again whole, as a series does (851-4151). A link query that fails is a series that can't be read: the page is read again, and after 3 runs it's held back.
    - **The Mac's pages use them** (851-4186), through `HealthDatabase`'s link queries (`PageQueries/LinkQueries.swift`), which 851-4187's tools share: `linkedSamples(ofWorkout:types:)`, `symptoms(ofElectrocardiogram:)` and `workoutEffort(of:)`.
      - **A reader tells linked from guessed by the record:** none is `LinkedSamples.notSynced` (an iPhone from before 851-4153, or links not synced yet), one whose `samples` is null is `tooMany` (only its `count` is known), one whose `samples` can't be read is taken for `notSynced`, and one with IDs is `linked`, its samples those that have arrived (`health_sample` records, so some may come later or be gone), in order of start, then ID. An empty record is linked to nothing: the workout had no samples added, which isn't a reason to guess. Effort records exist only for workouts with scores, so a workout without one has none, or hasn't synced them: nothing tells the two apart, and there was never a guess to fall back on.
      - **A workout's heart rate** (`HealthDatabase.workoutHeartRates(of:)`, for the tools too) is the heart rate linked to it, one source's (`mainSource`, should its app have added two devices' readings). Only for `notSynced` and `tooMany` is it the old guess, the readings during the workout of the source with the most, and then the chart's footer says so ("Readings by Apple Watch during the workout. Which readings belong to it hasn't synced from your iPhone yet.", or that it recorded too many). The range over the chart comes from the same samples; the averages in the summary are HealthKit's statistics, which never guessed. A record linked to no heart rate (an app that added none) charts none, as the ticket asked: the links say none was added. The page reads its heart rate and effort scores with `ThrottledFetch` of the samples and link tables (`workoutLinkTables`), so links, scores and samples that sync while it's open show.
      - **Its effort score** is a summary figure, "Effort" for one the person rated (`WorkoutEffortScore`) and "Est. Effort" for the Watch's (`EstimatedWorkoutEffortScore`), as Health rates it: "7 · Hard" (1–3 Easy, 4–6 Moderate, 7–8 Hard, 9–10 All Out). The score is the one HealthKit found most relevant, else, if that hasn't arrived, the latest the person rated that has, else the Watch's latest estimate (`HealthDatabase.effortScore`). A multisport workout lists its legs (its activities, each with its time), each with its own score, matched by the activity's ID (`WorkoutActivityRecord.id`, which the iPhone has uploaded since workouts first synced, 851-2565).
      - **An ECG's symptoms** are listed in a Symptoms Logged section at the end of its page, each by the name Health gives its type and how it was logged ("Present", as the ECG app logs them, or a severity), in order of start. Last because they're read after the page opens (a `ThrottledFetch` of the samples and symptoms tables, `electrocardiogramLinkTables`, so links that arrive while it's open show), so nothing moves when they do (851-3091, `LayoutShiftTests`). The Symptoms row still says "Symptoms Logged", "No Symptoms" or "Not Logged", and is all a recording whose links haven't synced, or whose symptoms haven't arrived, shows.
      - Workouts' pages hold 100 workouts (each with a query, and an hour's Watch run may link a few thousand IDs, all held until the page goes up) rather than 10,000. A link query that fails is logged with its error (`Backfill`). **Links that arrive after their workout or ECG** (should the Watch's sync deliver a workout before its samples, or an ECG before its symptoms) aren't read again, since the workout's anchor doesn't change: whether that happens needs a device (851-4190), which the Debug-only Diagnostics screen measures (below, Privacy).
  - Vision prescriptions (`health_vision_prescription`, 851-2572) keep the kind (glasses or contacts), when issued (as start and end) and expiring, a contacts brand, and each eye's lens as JSON: sphere, cylinder, axis and add power, plus glasses' vertex and pupillary distances and prism, or contacts' base curve and diameter, in diopters, degrees and millimeters. Health shares them one by one (per-object authorization), and may share an old one or stop sharing one at any time, which anchored queries don't see, so they're read whole every run, as characteristics are: they go up when they change, and one no longer shared is deleted. Scanned images (`HKAttachment`) aren't uploaded.
  - Medications (`health_medication`, 851-2571) keep the name Health shows, a nickname, the general form (`HKMedicationGeneralForm`'s raw string, any value), whether they're archived or scheduled, and their clinical codings as JSON. Their dose events (`health_medication_dose`) keep the medication, the log status and schedule type (HealthKit's raw values, any integer, so a status a later iOS adds isn't refused forever), the scheduled date and dose, the dose taken and its unit, with the usual times and provenance.
    - **Keyed by concept:** doses only name their medication's `HKHealthConceptIdentifier`, which has no public value but its domain. Its ID is the SHA-256 of the strings it archives (its domain and underlying identifier, collected through `NSKeyedArchiver`'s delegate, all public API), so the same concept gets the same ID on every run and iPhone. If a later iOS archived it differently, IDs would change once, which the sharing rules below absorb: the old medications and their doses are deleted, and the doses read again under the new IDs. Health keeps one medication per concept; if it ever had two, a current one wins over an archived one.
    - **Sharing:** Health shares medications one by one (per-object authorization), with their doses, and may share an old one or stop sharing one at any time. Medications aren't samples, so they're read whole at the start of every run of everything, before the types, and go up when they change (`uploadWhole`, as prescriptions do). Doses are samples, read through the anchored backfill like any type, so the thousands a person logs aren't sent again every run, and deletions come through the anchor. Two things an anchor doesn't see are handled with the medications:
      - A medication shared since may have doses from before the dose type's anchor. When the set of medications grows, the dose type's anchor is forgotten (and its count), so the same run reads every dose again; the server drops the unchanged ones. Only sharing more does this.
      - A medication no longer shared can't have its doses read, so their deletions never come. The iPhone keeps which doses it uploaded for each medication, in a backfill file (`medication-doses.json`) rather than the state, which is saved after every step. A page's doses are noted before it goes up (a page that fails is read again, and deleting a dose the server doesn't have does nothing), and deletions take them out. A medication no longer shared is deleted with all its doses, in one upload, before the state forgets it.
    - Upload Everything Again reads the doses from the start and keeps the medications' hash and IDs and the doses file, as it keeps prescriptions'. Signing out removes the file.
    - On the Mac, Browse dates both medication types by the latest dose. Medications counts as having data once one is shared, even without doses (an as-needed one never logged), undated.
    - **On the Mac** (851-2589): both Browse types open Medications: current ones, then archived ones, by the name they show (the nickname, else Health's), with the form and Scheduled or As Needed. A medication's page shows its adherence over the last 7 and 30 days (today and the days before, in the Mac's calendar): scheduled doses taken out of those scheduled, plus doses taken as needed. Each scheduled dose (medication and scheduled time) counts once, as its latest-logged event, should Health keep several. Below is the log, newest first, by the day a dose was due (when it was logged, for one taken as needed), each with its status (Taken, Skipped, Snoozed, Not Logged…), times and quantity, up to the latest 1,000 doses. The page shows the latest 100 at first, whole days, and Show More Doses adds 100 at a time (851-3004): it's a grouped form, which lays out every row rather than only those on screen, so a year of doses took seconds to open. There were no iPad reference screenshots to compare with.
  - Health Documents (`health_document`, 851-2836) are clinical documents in HL7's Clinical Document Architecture (CDA), such as a visit summary a provider sent, which Health lists with health records. Each keeps the title, patient, author and custodian names HealthKit extracts (possibly empty), with the usual times and provenance, and its XML as a blob, as Health keeps it (not compressed): they can be large, and the Mac's viewer (851-2837) shows them as they are.
    - **Sharing:** the document type is in the standard sheet, and then HealthKit asks about each document, the first time the app's document query (`HKDocumentQuery`) finds it, in a sheet of its own. Anchored queries list documents without their XML, and don't see one stop being shared, so documents are read whole every run of everything, like vision prescriptions (`HealthDocumentsReader`): which ones are shared (without their XML), then only those not uploaded yet with their XML, 10 to a page, each page's blobs before its rows. Documents never change, so the backfill keeps the IDs it uploaded (`documentIDs`) rather than a hash, and deletes one no longer shared. One too large for a blob (16 MB) is skipped and reported, and kept among the IDs so it isn't read again every run.
    - Nothing asks for documents but the run itself: the first run after Health access may show Health's sheet for them, and so may a later one that finds a new document. Whether a background run can show it isn't known, so documents are read last, after the Activity summaries, in case the query waits on it; if it fails, it's tried again on the next run.
    - **Consent** (decided with Alexandru, 2026-10-20): documents carry names, addresses and diagnoses, but the consent version stays `2026-10-15`. The consent already covers the Health data the person lets lil health read, and documents only sync when the person shares each one in Health's own sheet. The privacy policy lists them.
    - **On the Mac:** `healthDocuments`, synced like any entity. Health Documents opens a list, newest first, and each document a viewer (851-2837): the patient, author, custodian and date HealthKit extracted, then each section of the structured body (its `<title>` and narrative `<text>`) as a form section titled by its header (851-3004), with paragraphs, lists and tables. The XML downloads when a document opens, through the blob cache, as ECG voltages do. `ClinicalDocument` (`HealthDatabase`) reads it with Foundation's `XMLParser`, in the CDA namespace only; coded entries and images aren't shown. In C-CDA nearly every section's entries repeat its narrative, so only a section with entries but no narrative, or an image (`renderMultiMedia`, `observationMedia`), makes the page say some of the document isn't shown. Narrative elements it doesn't know show their text; superscripts and subscripts stay raised and lowered (10⁹, not 109), and spanning table cells keep later cells in their columns. The XML is decoded in its own encoding (byte order mark, then the declaration), and only as text when it's shown. A document no longer shared says so. Malformed XML, or a document without readable sections (an unstructured `nonXMLBody`, a PDF say), shows its XML instead, saying why; Show XML opens it for any document, in a text view rather than SwiftUI's `Text`, since documents can be megabytes.
  - **On the Mac:** Vision Prescriptions is a row in Body Measurements, where Health lists it (851-3187): a list, newest first, with expiry, and each prescription's Right (OD) and Left (OS) values, leaving out rows neither eye has (851-2592).
  - **No Health Details on the Mac** (851-3187, decided with Alexandru, 2026-10-23): the characteristics (date of birth, sex, blood type, Fitzpatrick skin type, wheelchair use) rarely change and don't need a page of their own, so the Mac shows them nowhere. Health Details, below Summary in the sidebar since 851-2592, went, and links to it were removed rather than redirected: search, the Open intent and Spotlight, and `lil-health open` no longer offer it or the characteristics. The characteristics still sync, stay in the data export, and `lil-health details` and the `health_details` tool read them.
  - Characteristics (date of birth, sex, blood type…) and Activity summaries have no anchors, so every run of everything reads them. Characteristics go up when their content changes (a hash in the backfill's state). Activity summaries go up all at once the first time, then the last 30 days', whose numbers and goals can still change (e.g. when a Watch that was away syncs). A characteristic that stops being readable is deleted. A locked phone pauses the run rather than looking like removed characteristics.
  - **Upload Everything Again** (851-2662), in the iPhone's Settings sheet with consent: each type's anchor says everything before it is on the server, so history Health starts sharing later (iOS 27's "Past 30 Days and Future Data", widened to All Recorded Data in Settings) would never go up. After a confirmation, `HealthBackfill.uploadAgain()` first catches up from the anchors (so deletions since go up), then forgets only how far each type was read, the counts (which the new pass makes again) and when Activity summaries were read, and the app runs it from the start. What deletes records no longer readable (characteristics', prescriptions' IDs), the last upload and the account's rate and storage limits stay: signing out's `reset()` forgets those too. The server keeps one copy of each record, but the iPhone sends everything again, blobs included, so the alert says it can use a lot of data. Detecting a limited history (recent samples, none older than 30 days) to suggest it is left for later.
  - **Health access in the Settings sheet** (851-2775): Health doesn't let apps open their access settings (the app's own Settings page has no Health permissions), so the sheet says where they are, Settings › Privacy & Security › Health › lil health (checked against iOS 27's Settings, 851-3113: Health itself moved under Settings › Apps in iOS 18, so the onboarding's Settings › Health › Data Access & Devices was wrong; the onboarding and the main screen's Categories footer now name the same path), and offers Health's pickers for what's shared one by one, Choose Medications… and Choose Vision Prescriptions…, after which the app uploads what's newly shared.
- **Statistics:**
  - **Cumulative types** (steps, energy, distance…): the iPhone computes 30-minute sums with HealthKit's own statistics queries, so de-duplication by source priority (e.g. iPhone and Watch steps) matches Health exactly.
    - 30-minute buckets keep days exact in half-hour time zones. Only offsets ending in :45 are approximate.
    - The Mac adds buckets up into days in its current time zone.
  - **Discrete types** (heart rate, weight…): the Mac computes averages, minimums and maximums from raw samples. Averages of bucket averages would be wrong, and HealthKit's statistics don't include sample counts to weight them.
  - Raw samples also back "Show All Data".
  - **On the Mac** (`HealthDatabase`):
    - Cumulative totals come from the statistics source's 30-minute buckets, placed by their start in the Mac's time zone.
    - Discrete averages follow each type's aggregation style:
      - **Arithmetic:** a plain average.
      - **Time-weighted:** each sample counts until its source's next sample, up to an hour, and a source's last one for its own duration. Sources are weighted separately, so a second device's readings don't cut the first's short.
      - **Sound levels:** an equivalent continuous level.
    - The statistics source is the Watch-paired iPhone among those that reported in the last 14 days, then the most recent of those. If none reported recently, it's the most recent of all. Ties go to the higher device ID.
- **Multiple iPhones:** every signed-in iPhone uploads, relying on Health's iCloud sync to give them the same data.
  - Samples de-duplicate by HealthKit UUID.
  - Statistics are stored per iPhone, and the Mac reads one statistics source: the iPhone paired with an Apple Watch, otherwise the most recently active one. An iPhone that's behind on iCloud sync can't overwrite a complete one.
    - Each iPhone's statistics are `health_statistic` rows, keyed by device, type and bucket. Its `health_statistics_device` row records whether it's paired with a Watch and when it last reported, and the Mac picks the source from those rows.
    - Both entities are device-scoped (`docs/sync/protocol.md`): the server stamps rows with the uploading device.
    - An iPhone that signs out has its device row deleted, and its device-scoped rows are deleted from the store a chunk at a time (`docs/sync/protocol.md`). So a signed-out Watch-paired iPhone can't stay the source. One that stops without signing out (app deleted, tokens expired) is retired by a daily job once it has no live session and hasn't been seen for a day. Until then, the Mac's 14-day rule skips it.
  - Not supported: Health iCloud sync turned off, or iPhones on different Apple IDs in one lil account. Signing in on a second iPhone shows a warning about this: after sign-in, the app checks `GET /v1/me`'s `devices` once per account for another `health-ios` install.
- **Recorded time zones** (851-4155, after the sync audit of 2026-11-09; Alexandru: "why do we ignore it? sounds like we shouldn't"): records keep the zone they were recorded in (`HKMetadataKeyTimeZone`, kept in their metadata as `HKTimeZone`, 851-4152). **Nights follow it, as in Apple Health. Everything else stays on the viewer's day and clock.** Until then nothing read the key: every engine put every record in one zone, the viewer's. That wasn't a decision: the engines were built around one calendar per run.
  - **What Apple Health does** (iOS 27.2, checked in a simulator with synthetic samples recorded in Tokyo and across New York and London, viewed from Los Angeles):
    - HealthKit keeps the key only when the app saving a record gives it. It adds none itself, not even through the workout builder. Apple's Watch writes it on workouts and sleep, and most readings (steps, heart rate) have none.
    - **Sleep follows it.** A night slept in Tokyo (23:00 to 06:30 JST) is Tokyo's night and clock in Sleep's day, week and month charts. A night whose records changed zone (New York, then London) is drawn whole on one clock, the zone it ended in.
    - **Nothing else does.** Charts, totals, lists, and a sample's start and end are in the viewer's zone: a run at 07:00 JST on Friday 6 November showed on Thursday the 5th at 2 PM, Los Angeles' clock. A sample's details add a Time Zone row ("Japan Standard Time").
  - **Match Apple Health** (Alexandru, 2026-11-10). The design merged first (#504) went further, following the zone for workouts and readings too. He chose Health's behavior instead: then a day never mixes zones (a Tokyo run and its steps are on the same day), and what the apps show matches Health's.
  - **The rule:**
    - **A record's zone** is its `HKTimeZone` when that's a name the tools' `timeZone` takes (the Health toolkit's Time zones, below: one of the listed IANA names, in its proper case, which the engine's platform knows). Otherwise, or without the key, it has none. Apple writes `TimeZone.current.identifier`, an IANA name. HealthKit refuses to save a record whose zone is a UTC offset (`+09:00`), in the wrong case or unknown, but takes `PST` and `GMT+9`, which another app could write: those records have no zone, on every engine (851-4184).
    - **Nights:** a night's zone is the zone of the last-ending of its records that have one, else the viewer's. That zone names the night's day (records starting from 6 PM count toward the next day), decides the noon rule, and places the night on Sleep's clock (`SleepClock`: at its start's clock time, then its true length, so a night across a change of zone stays one unbroken column, as it does across a daylight saving change). A night's zone must be known before its day, so each source's records are first split into runs where sleep stops for 3 hours or more, in absolute time, and each run takes the zone of the last-ending of its records that have one. The night rules then run as before. Nights recorded in one zone, or in none, are exactly what they were.
    - **Everything else is in the viewer's zone:** readings, workouts, totals, times of day, workout windows, today and now, on every engine. A sleep window's readings still go to their night's day, which is now its zone's.
    - **Display:** the Mac shows a Time Zone row in a sample's and a workout's details when they have one, as Health does (851-4179, `RecordedTimeZone`): the zone's long name, standard or daylight as at the record's start ("Japan Standard Time", "British Summer Time"; `UTC` as written, "Coordinated Universal Time", where Foundation would say Greenwich), and when its offset then isn't the Mac's, the start on its clock below the title ("Started 8/14/2024, 6:30 AM local time"). The Start and End rows stay on the Mac's clock, and a name the tools wouldn't take (`+09:00`) gets no row. Sleep shows a night on its zone's clock.
    - **Tools:** nights follow the rule: a night's `date` is on its zone's clock. Every time field stays in the call's `timeZone`, so times compare across records. A record with a zone adds `recordedTimeZone`, the name as the record gave it, and the same instants on that zone's clock, with its offset: `recordedStart` and `recordedEnd` on `samples`' items (a series' values each with their own), `workouts`, `workout`, `workout_routes`, `workout_route` and `sleep`'s nights (the night's zone), `electrocardiograms`' recordings (851-4159) and `audiograms` (851-4161), and `recordedTime` on `state_of_mind`'s logs and `assessments`, which have one `time` (851-4177; 851-4166 had added a raw `recordedTimeZone` to `samples`). They're optional keys, left out without a zone, or with a name the tools' `timeZone` wouldn't take (`+09:00`, which `samples` used to echo), on both servers alike (the conformance cases, `RecordedTimes` in `HealthTools` and the server's `RecordedTimes.ts`). A workout's activities, pauses and laps, and a route's points, don't repeat them: the workout's or route's offset gives their clock.
  - **Engines:** the cards' core (`HealthCards`) groups nights, and the Mac's Sleep page and the `sleep` tools use it. Then each engine reads its sleep records' zones (`cards.md`, Recorded time zones). Only sleep records are read, a few thousand a year, so it costs next to nothing.
  - **Data already stored:** nothing needs uploading again. The iPhone has always sent `HKTimeZone` (851-4152 kept it in the allow-list), so every record on the server and the Macs already has it. Each app computes from its own data, so a Mac on an older version keeps its nights in its own zone until it updates. Agents' answers change when the server deploys.
  - **Tests:** the cards' conformance cases have a June 2025 trip (`cards.md`). Readings and a run recorded in Tokyo stay on London's days and clock. Those cases pass on every engine already, and keep it that way. A night in Tokyo, and a night across a change of zone, are marked `pending` on each engine until its ticket makes them pass. The tools' cases come with 851-4177.
  - **Tickets**, in order: 851-4174 (nights in the core and the Mac's engines), then 851-4175 (the server's) and 851-4176 (the iPhone's), then 851-4177 (the tools' recorded times, on both servers) and 851-4178 (the Mac's Sleep page). 851-4179 (the Time Zone row in the Mac's details) can go any time.
- **Privacy:**
  - Health data is readable by the server and encrypted at rest by Cloudflare. That keeps server-side features possible, such as the remote MCP server below.
  - It's processed only with explicit consent (GDPR special-category data), and never used for ads or sold.
    - **Consent** is to a privacy policy version, its effective date (`healthConsentPolicyVersion` in `src/core/Consents.ts`). The server records each consent and withdrawal in D1's `consents` table with its time, as proof of consent, and deletes them with the account.
    - `GET /v1/me/consents/health` returns the required version and the consent in force. `POST` with the version the app showed records consent; an older version gets 409 `ConsentOutdated`. `DELETE` withdraws it.
    - New batch uploads without a current consent fail with 403 `ConsentRequired`, before anything reaches R2. A batch the store already knows still answers with its status, and batches accepted before a withdrawal are still applied. Data already uploaded stays until the account is deleted.
    - Bumping the version, for a change in how Health data is used, refuses uploads until each user consents again.
    - **On the iPhone,** the consent screen comes after sign-in and before Health access: what's uploaded, where and why, a privacy policy link, and Agree or Not Now. Nothing is pre-checked. `HealthConsentClient.policyVersion` is the version the screen describes, so bump it with the screen.
      - The iPhone remembers the version it consented to (`healthConsentPolicyVersion` in app storage). Nothing uploads without it, including HealthKit's background wakes and background tasks. Each launch asks the server before uploading, which catches consent withdrawn or given on another iPhone (offline, the iPhone's answer stands). An upload refused with `ConsentRequired` clears it too, in the background as well. An answer that was out while consent was given or withdrawn here is dropped as stale.
      - The screen comes up on its own once per sign-in. After Not Now or a withdrawal, the home screen says nothing is uploading and offers it again.
      - Withdraw Consent in the account section clears the iPhone's consent and pauses uploads at once, tells the server, then offers to delete the account. If the server can't be told, consent stands again and uploading stays paused until the next change or launch.
  - **The iPhone's logs show no values** (851-4183, after the server's, 851-4054): the backfill's diagnostics (`BackfillLog`, the device's log as `tech.lil.health` › `Backfill`, public, and `HealthBackfill/diagnostics.log`) are type names, counts, batch IDs, timings and which step failed. Errors go through one helper, `BackfillLog.describe` (`write(_:error:)`), which logs an error as its type and codes: `ClientError`'s operation ID, status and underlying error; a decoding or encoding error's case, type and coding path, with only the keys a type declares (its `CodingKeys`) and array indexes, any other key (a dictionary's, which is data even when it reads as a name) as `<key>`; a Cocoa error's domain and code (never its user info); a Swift error's type and case, and for our own modules' errors only the fields named in `keptFields` (counts, status codes, entity and type names), the rest as their type. A type's failure in a run is logged as it happens (`StepCount failed: …`). Never a description: `ClientError`'s holds the operation's input, the request and response, `EncodingError.invalidValue`'s the value. A server's batch failure message, which can quote a line of the batch (V8's JSON errors), is the line's `privateDetail`: private in the device's log, `<private>` in the file. The continued upload's log (`ContinuedUpload`) leaves out a failed run's reason, an error's description for the task's UI, and logs the error the same way; so does the simulator's synthetic data (`SyntheticHealth`). The Mac app has no loggers of its own, and `reportIssue` logs its message private. Tests (`BackfillLogErrorTests`) feed each kind of error synthetic values and check none reaches the line.
  - **Diagnostics, in Debug builds only** (851-4173, 851-4190): Settings › Diagnostics, compiled out of Release builds (`#if DEBUG`, `DiagnosticsView` and `HealthDiagnostics`) and hidden in snapshot tests, runs checks a developer needs their own iPhone's real data for, and shows counts and durations only: never a value, date, name, source or ID. Nothing is uploaded, logged or saved, and the numbers are gone when the screen closes; the developer reads them back. **Quantity Series** reads every sample of each quantity type, a page at a time, and counts samples, series (`count` over 1), their values together and the largest. **Link Order** checks the last 90 days' workouts and ECGs with symptoms: HealthKit doesn't say when it added an object, but its anchors give the order, of any type, so a workout's own anchor (an anchored query for it alone; limited to 1, it finds no workout at all in the iOS 27.2 simulator) passed to a query of its linked samples finds those added after it (`HealthKitDiagnosticsTests`, with links added after their workout through the deprecated `HKHealthStore.addSamples(_:to:)`; `HKWorkoutBuilder`'s own samples come before its workout). It can be wrong both ways: a sync that adds both in one transaction adds them in some order, though no read falls between them, and a sample saved before its workout but linked to it later keeps its place, so it isn't counted. **New Workouts and ECGs** is the ticket's own measurement: watching from the moment it starts (an anchored query's updates, the screen kept awake), it counts each arrival's links when it arrives and again at 15 s, 30 s, 1, 2 and 5 minutes, which is what a read at each moment would have found.
    - **What could leak before:** every error was interpolated whole, as public text. Nothing found leaked in practice (a `ClientError`'s request is the one before the auth middleware adds the token, bodies print as `HTTPBody` without contents, inputs are IDs), but nothing stopped it: an operation whose input holds a value, an `EncodingError.invalidValue`, an underlying error quoting data, or a server's batch failure that quotes a line would have reached the device's log, and sysdiagnoses, as public text.
  - **Metadata: what's kept** (851-4152, after the sync audit of 2026-11-09): HealthKit metadata used to be copied through whole, so device serial numbers, UDI lot and serial numbers, digital signatures, other apps' record IDs and own keys, and `HKDevice`'s local identifiers were stored and synced, though nothing used them, and agents would have seen them once tools pass metadata on (851-4156). Now an **allow-list**: the Swift `HealthCatalog.healthKitMetadataKeys` decides each of HealthKit's keys, kept or dropped, and `keptMetadata` keeps only the kept ones. It's the source of truth, written into `health-catalog.json` (`metadataKeys`, `deviceProperties`) for the server, and `HealthCatalogSDKTests` fails until a key a new SDK declares is decided, and checks each key's value against HealthKit's constant (many aren't the constant's name: `HKMetadataKeyTimeZone` is `HKTimeZone`).
    - **The rule:** keep what says what a value means or how it was measured, which the apps use or the tools will (851-4156). Drop identifiers (of a device, a unit, a lot, another app's record or catalog entry), signatures and sync bookkeeping, which nothing needs. **Every key that isn't HealthKit's is dropped**: other apps' own keys (anything an app writes, from notes to account IDs), and Apple's private ones. The only exception is lil health's own `LilWorkoutPlan` (851-4030). The made-up `HKAudioExposureLevel` and `HKAudioExposureDuration` the Mac read until 851-4148 aren't kept: HealthKit's are `HKMetadataKeyAudioExposureLevel` and `…Duration`. A key a newer SDK adds is dropped until it's listed. Nested metadata is filtered the same way: a workout's activities' and events'.
    - **Kept (63 of HealthKit's 71):** on every object, the recorded time zone (`HKTimeZone`: nights' days and clocks after travel, a Time Zone row in details, and the tools' recorded times; 851-4155) and whether it was entered by hand (`HKWasUserEntered`). The device's name and manufacturer (as `HKDevice`'s, which are kept), whether it was calibrated and where it was worn (`HKMetadataKeyAppleDeviceCalibrated`, `HKMetadataKeyDevicePlacementSide`). How an estimate was made: algorithm versions (`HKAlgorithmVersion`, the ECG's), the earliest data used, session estimates, values clamped to a sensor's bounds, reference ranges and lab readings, motion context, barometric pressure, water salinity, daylight's lux, physical effort's estimation type and the activity it was during. Heart: motion context, sensor location, the high and low heart rate events' thresholds, heart rate recovery's test, activity, duration and maximum, VO2 max's test type, value and low cardio fitness threshold. Hearing: exposure level, duration and headphone gain. Clinical, cycle and food: blood glucose meal time, insulin delivery reason (basal or bolus), body temperature sensor location, the cycle's start, sexual activity's protection (the sample's meaning, sensitive as the sample is), the food's name and the glasses prescription's description. Workouts: indoor or outdoor, swimming location, brand name, elevation, speeds, METs, lap length, stroke style, SWOLF, slope grade, weather, coached, group fitness, a Fitness+ session, gym machines' distance and duration, and FTP test type.
    - **Dropped (8):** `HKDeviceSerialNumber`, `HKUDIDeviceIdentifier` (the device's model under the UDI system, which `model` already says) and `HKUDIProductionIdentifier` (lot and serial number), `HKDigitalSignature` (a signed record of a medical device's reading, which nothing verifies), `HKMetadataKeySyncIdentifier` and `HKMetadataKeySyncVersion` (another app's own record ID and its version, for HealthKit's dedup; ours is the UUID), `HKExternalUUID` (another app's ID for the record) and `HKMetadataKeyAppleFitnessPlusCatalogIdentifier` (which Fitness+ workout, with no public way to name it; whether it was a Fitness+ session is kept).
    - **The device:** `HKDevice`'s name, manufacturer, model and hardware, firmware and software versions are kept (`HealthCatalog.keptDeviceProperties`); its `localIdentifier` (often a serial number or Bluetooth address) and `udiDeviceIdentifier` are dropped. Source names and bundle IDs stay: they're what sample detail, Data Sources, the cards' `sources` filter and the tools show.
    - **Where it's enforced:** the iPhone's encoder uploads only what's kept (`HealthEncoder.metadata` and `provenance(of:)`), so its `health_provenance` records get new IDs (a hash of what they hold). The server drops the rest from what it's sent (`HealthIngestion`'s `parseLine`, `src/health/HealthMetadata.ts`), since installed iPhones from before keep uploading everything until they update. The Mac stores what the server serves, so it filters nothing itself.
    - **Records stored before: a cleanup pass on the server, synced to the Macs** (`src/health/MetadataCleanup.ts`). Each store's alarm goes through every record with metadata or a device (the 13 entities `metadataFields` finds), 5,000 at a time 5 seconds apart (as retiring a device does, so a large store isn't kept busy and its Macs don't pull every second), in primary key order, and rewrites those that change through the change log, merging only the cleaned fields, so the Macs get the clean records with their next pull as any other change, and the snapshots written from then on hold them. No new epoch, so no Mac bootstraps its whole copy again, and no re-upload from the iPhone. Deleted records still in a table are left alone (an upsert would bring them back), and go when their tombstones are swept. So is a record a check made stricter since refuses (`checkRow`), counted in the pass's closing log line, rather than holding the pass up. Clean metadata, `{}` included, isn't rewritten. The store records the version it was cleaned for (`metadata_policy`, `metadataPolicyVersion`), and its start sets the alarm while it's behind, so a store nobody writes to is cleaned too; a new store starts clean. **Bump `metadataPolicyVersion` whenever a kept key or property is dropped**, so every store goes through its records again, and after rolling the server back past 851-4152 (older code stores everything again, in stores already marked clean); keeping more needs nothing, since what's dropped is gone (the iPhone's Upload Everything Again sends it again). Older copies end on their own: snapshots from before are replaced within a week of the cleanup and deleted a week after (`docs/sync/protocol.md`), and changes from before 851-2676 that held a copy of their row are compacted after 30 days.   - **Metadata: quantities and dates go up whole** (851-4150, after the sync audit of 2026-11-09): the iPhone used to write a metadata `HKQuantity` as its `description`, which keeps 6 significant digits (`1234.56789 m` was `1234.57 m`, `123456789 m` `1.23457e+08 m`) and writes a percent ×100 ("45.67 %" for a fraction of 0.4567) and METs with a middle dot (`kcal/hr·kg`), and a date as bare Unix milliseconds, which look like any other number. That rounded elevation, speeds, METs, weather, lap lengths, heart rate thresholds, sound levels and VO2 max for the tools and the Mac.
    - **The forms:** a quantity is `{ "value": 84.6012345, "unit": "m" }` in its key's unit, declared with the key in the allow-list (`HealthMetadataKey.quantityUnit`, `HealthCatalog.metadataQuantityUnits`: meters, m/s, `kcal/hr·kg`, `degC`, `count/min`, seconds, `dBASPL`, `mL/min·kg`, `lx`, `kPa`, and `%`), written as `HKUnit`'s `unitString` writes it (a test checks each round-trips, and another that the tools read each key's quantity in its unit). A session estimate (`HKMetadataKeySessionEstimate`) is in its sample's catalog unit, since it's a reading of the sample's own kind. The value is `doubleValue(for:)` to 12 significant digits (`HealthCatalog.significant`, as quantity series' values, 851-4149, and the server's `significant`), which drops a conversion's noise (59 °F is 15 °C, not 15.000000000000057) and keeps far more than any sensor measures. **A percent is a fraction**, as `HKUnit` holds it (0.72 for 72%); readers multiply it by 100 to read it as a description did, so Apple's Workout app's humidity, 72 percent units for 72%, still reads 72 after the ×100 fix (`HealthMetadataValues.Quantity.humidity`). Both servers read the text from its fields with the same arithmetic, not a date parser (Foundation rolls a 60th second over, JavaScript refuses it), so a field out of range is none on both. Objects rather than strings like `"84.6012345 m"`, so nothing needs parsing, and nothing reads a date as a number.

Arguments outside the schema are a tool error too, in the remote server's words, every issue at once (851-4034, the Health toolkit above), checked before the call is logged; until 851-4034 they were the request's error (`-32602`, invalid params). A string the SDK's `Value` decodes as data (a data URL) is still a string, as it is to the remote server.
  - **Access:** every call checks and logs (`AgentAccessStore.authorize`) before it opens the database, as a command does: the tool's name and the arguments it takes as JSON (`metric {"range":"W","type":"Steps"}`; others an agent sends are left out, as the tool ignores them), with the agent as the process. Listing the tools and reading the catalog don't need access, so an agent connects while it's off, and each call then says where to turn it on. The database is opened for each call, so a server running since before an update reads once the app has migrated.
  - **Resource:** `health://catalog`, the catalog of categories and types with workout activities' names (`HealthCatalog.json()`, the same as `Services/api/health-catalog.json`), served whether or not access is on, since it isn't the person's data. The remote server should serve it under the same URI.
  - **Through `lil mcp`** (851-2897), which combines every app's server, its tools are `health.summary` and so on, and its parent process is `lil`. - **App actions** (851-2846, decided with Alexandru on 2026-10-21): agents and scripts can make lil health for Mac do things without touching its database: open a page, sync now and export. They're App Intents, which bring Shortcuts, Siri, Spotlight and the `shortcuts` command with them. macOS only: the iPhone app is a bridge with no pages, and the switch only exists on the Mac.
  - **Open in lil health** (`OpenHealthPageIntent`, 851-2892), an `OpenIntent` whose target is a `HealthPage` (an `AppEntity`): Summary, a category, or a data type in the catalog with a category. Characteristics (Blood Type, Date of Birth…), which have none, aren't pages, nor is Health Details since it went (851-3187): a Shortcut saved with `health-details` finds no page. IDs are `summary`, `category:<raw value>` and `type:<HealthKit identifier>`, with the sidebar's names, symbols and colors (drawn into the icon, since an intent's symbol images can't be tinted on the Mac). A type's subtitle is where it opens: its category. Its query matches names as the sidebar's search does (`localizedStandardContains`), exact names first, and types also by the names `lil-health` accepts (`HealthCatalog.type(named:)`, shared with `HealthTools.dataType`). It suggests Summary and the categories. It brings the main window forward, clears the search, and selects the page; a type opens as if its row were clicked in its category (the category, with its metric pushed: `AppFeature.State.metricPath`, the detail's navigation path). Whatever was pushed goes: every stack's path is the store's, so opening a category while one of its metrics shows goes back to the category itself (851-3030). The sidebar's search finds the same types; clearing the search selects the found type's category. Each stack writes its path back with the selection it shows (`metricPathChanged`), and one that's no longer selected is ignored: the stack going away as the selection changes writes an empty path, which would otherwise undo the metric just opened. A SwiftUI `Window` app quits when its window closes, so there's no running app without its window to bring back.
  - **Sync lil health** (`SyncHealthDataIntent`, 851-2893): on the Mac, syncing means pulling the account's changes from the server now; the Mac never uploads Health data, the iPhone does, and the intent can't make it upload. Its description says only that it gets the latest data from the iPhone, and what it needs (851-3113): the edge case isn't something people decide on. It's useful right before an agent reads through `lil-health` or `lil mcp`. `AppFeature` performs `HealthAppAction.sync` as Try Again does, a new pull that cuts a failure's backoff short or the idle wait between pulls, unless a pull is running (`.idle` before the first since signing in, `.syncing`, `.bootstrapping`): that one goes on, since restarting it would throw away a bootstrap's progress, and an agent may sync before every read. Then it waits for the status to say how the pull ended: caught up answers with when (`.synced(at:)`), failed or offline throws the sync's message (`HealthAppActionError.syncFailed`, `.offline`). It waits at most 60 s (`HealthAppActions.syncWait`), long enough for a usual catch-up but not a big bootstrap; still syncing then isn't an error, and answers "Still syncing. lil health will finish in the background." So the status before the pull isn't taken for its outcome, the action reads it first and restarts the pull after, and the sync engine announces a failure again when a pull fails as the last one did (`LilSyncEngine.setStatus`). It runs in the background only (`supportedModes` `.background`), never bringing a running app's window forward (one that wasn't running is launched to ask, with its window, as for Open), and returns how it went as text, with no dialog (below): "lil health is up to date." or "Still syncing. lil health will finish in the background." The log shows `sync`.
  - **Export lil health Data** (`ExportHealthDataIntent`, 851-2894): the account's data export (`accounts.md`, Exporting data), saved into a folder without the save panel, and returned as an `IntentFile`, so a shortcut can pass it on and `shortcuts run … --output-path` writes it. The folder is optional and defaults to Downloads. `AppFeature` hands `HealthAppAction.export(to:)` to the Settings pane's `DataExportFeature`, so Settings shows its progress, one export runs at a time ("An export is already running."), and signing out stops it. A folder that doesn't exist or can't be written to fails at once (`folderNotFound`, `folderNotWritable`); the export's own failures throw the message Settings shows (`exportFailed`), and Cancel in Settings fails it too (`exportCancelled`). It's a `ProgressReportingIntent`: the app reports preparing, then the download's share (`HealthAppActionProgress`, passed with each request beside its reply). The log shows `export <folder>`, with `~` for the home folder (`export ~/Downloads`). It runs in the background only, returns no dialog (below), and has no App Shortcut: an export is something a shortcut passes on, not something people ask Siri for. The privacy policy covers it (851-2891): what the shortcut does with the archive is up to it.
    - **How long it waits:** for the archive, however long the server takes (`DataExportFeature` gives up after 70 minutes). Checked on macOS 27 with a Debug build whose export was held for an hour: `shortcuts run` was still waiting after 37 minutes, with no timeout from Shortcuts or the system, and the intent was never cancelled. Stopping the run (ending `shortcuts run`) ended the shortcut's action (`NSUserCancelledError`) but not the app's `perform`, and the export went on in the app, showing in Settings; it saves to the folder when it's done, since the export doesn't depend on the intent. If the system does cancel the intent once the export has started, it throws "lil health is still exporting. It'll be saved to Downloads." (`stillExporting`). Exports are usually ready within minutes: the server waits out a bulk upload at most an hour.
  - **No dialogs** (851-2894, 851-2943): intents that scripts run don't return a dialog (`ProvidesDialog`). Shortcuts waits for a dialog's Done before the shortcut goes on, so `shortcuts run` doesn't return until someone clicks it (seen with Export's first version). They return a value instead: Export its file (`ReturnsValue<IntentFile>`), Sync its outcome as text (`ReturnsValue<String>`), which the Shortcuts editor shows under the action when it runs there, a shortcut can show with Show Result, and `shortcuts run` writes out. Errors still show, and `shortcuts run` exits with them at once (status 1, the message on stderr). One thing still waits, and it's Shortcuts' own: the first time `shortcuts run` runs a shortcut whose last action returns something, Shortcuts asks on screen whether to allow its output ("Allow “…” to output 1 text item?"), and the run waits for the answer. Always Allow makes it a one-time question; a shortcut that ends with another action (Nothing, say) isn't asked. Checked on macOS 27 (851-2943): a shortcut of Sync then Nothing returned at once, three times in a row, with `sync` logged each time. App Intents in Xcode 27 has nothing that shows only when someone's watching: an `IntentResult` is a value, a dialog, a snippet or an intent to open, decided by `perform`'s type, and `systemContext` only says whether Siri is voice-only (`isVoiceOnly`), not whether a person started the run. Siri and Spotlight, which said Sync's dialog, now get the text without one; how they present it wasn't checked (neither can be driven from a script). A second, dialog-only intent for the Siri phrase was ruled out: it would put a dialog back in any shortcut built from the App Shortcut.
  - **Testing in Shortcuts** (851-2894): Shortcuts looks an action up by the app's bundle ID, so with several copies of `tech.lil.health.debug` registered with LaunchServices (every DerivedData folder's Debug build, 851-3027) it can read another copy's App Intents metadata: the library lists the actions, but one the other copy lacks is added as "Unknown Action". Unregister the other Debug copies (`lsregister -u <path>`; `lsregister -dump | grep 'lil health.app'` lists them), register the one under test (`lsregister -f`), and relaunch Shortcuts. Run the app in a temporary home (`CFFIXED_USER_HOME`) against a preview's test account, with access turned on in that home's `AgentAccess.sqlite`.
  - **App Shortcuts:** "Open (page) in lil health", "Show (page) in lil health" and "Sync lil health"; none for Export. `LilHealthAppShortcuts` lives in the app target: Xcode extracts a package's App Shortcuts into the package's metadata, but leaves them out of the app's.
  - **Where the code is:** the intents and entities in `HealthMacApp/Intents/`, which provides `HealthMacAppIntents: AppIntentsPackage`, included by the app target's own `AppIntentsPackage` (`LilHealthAppIntents`). The metadata extractor picks those up from the package.
  - **How intents reach the app:** the `HealthAppActions` dependency, `perform(_ action: HealthAppAction, _ progress: HealthAppActions.Progress) async throws -> HealthAppActionResult`. `AppFeature` reads its `requests()` stream while signed in, where each request carries its reply and where to report progress (`report(_:)`, heard until it's answered), and performs it with the same reducer actions as the UI (`.pageOpened`). Signing out ends the stream and fails what it hadn't answered. Signed out, or not signed in within 10 s of launch (an intent may launch the app before the stored session is checked), an action fails with "Sign in to lil health first." The command line's requests (851-2895, below) use the same path.
  - **Access, every time:** each intent first calls `AgentAccessStore.authorize(command:process:)`, as the command does before a query, with the action as the command (`open Steps`, `open Summary`) and `Shortcuts, Siri or Spotlight` as the process, since App Intents doesn't say which one called (`HealthAppIntents.perform`). While access is off the intent does nothing else, and fails with `AgentAccessError.off`'s message: it runs in the background until access is checked (`supportedModes` `[.background, .foreground(.dynamic)]`), so a running app isn't brought forward, though one that wasn't running has been launched to ask. Siri's and Spotlight's runs are checked too: an intent can't tell a person from an agent's shortcut. So while the setting is off (the default), Siri's "Open Steps in lil health" answers with how to turn it on. Settings › Agents says the setting also covers Shortcuts, Siri and Spotlight, and its name, Allow access on this Mac (851-3114), doesn't single out agents.
  - **The command line's requests** (851-2895): `lil-health open`, `sync` and `export` (851-2896) ask the app through `AgentAccess.sqlite`, and the app performs them through `HealthAppActions`, as the intents' are, so they behave the same.
    - **Not through the intents:** no public API lets a process run another app's App Intent (`shortcuts run` only runs a shortcut the person saved, by its name).
    - **A row is the permission:** each request is a row in `AgentAccess.sqlite`'s `actionRequests` table, which only a process running as the person can write, the same trust as the switch (a consent switch, not a sandbox). `AgentAccessStore.requestAction(_:process:)` checks access and logs the request in one transaction, as `authorize` does, with the intents' command text (`open Steps`, `sync`, `export ~/Downloads`; `AgentAction.command`, checked against `HealthAppAction.command` for every page) and the parent process, as the queries log it. Then it adds the row: a random 128-bit ID, the action as JSON (`{"action": "open", "page": "type:…"}`, `{"action": "sync"}`, `{"action": "export", "folder": "/Users/…"}`; pages by `HealthPage.id`), when it was asked, and the process that asked.
    - **The signal carries nothing:** a running app is woken by an empty distributed notification, `tech.lil.health.actionRequested` (`AgentActionSignal`, posted to deliver at once, even to an app in the background); without a running app, the command launches it, and the app looks for requests when it launches (`AppFeature`'s `.task`, signed in or not). It listens before it first looks, so a request made in between isn't missed.
    - **No URL scheme:** any web page, document or app can open a URL, so a `tech.lil.health…://export` link would let a web page start an export or bring the app forward. With the row as the permission, a URL would only be a signal, which the notification already is, without a scheme anyone can call.
    - **Claiming** (`claimActions`): the requests no app has claimed, at most 60 s old (`claimWindow`), marked claimed by the app's process in the same transaction, so two app instances or a relaunch can't run one twice. While access is off it claims nothing and fails what's waiting ("access is off"), so a request made just before access was turned off never runs. A request whose JSON this version can't read (a newer command's) fails with "Update lil health". Then `AppFeature` performs each one through `HealthAppActions.perform`, which waits for sign-in after launch and fails with "Sign in to lil health first." (`signedOut`), and writes the outcome (`finish(id:outcome:)`): `ok` with the result as JSON (done, synced at, still syncing, exported path), or `failed` with a reason (`accessOff`, `signedOut`, `appQuit`, `failed`) and the message the intents show, so 851-2896 can pick its exit status without reading messages. Only the first answer counts.
    - **Stale rows,** swept by each request and claim: unclaimed requests older than 60 s (or dated more than 60 s ahead, after the clock was set back) are deleted, so an app that wasn't running doesn't act on them later; outcomes are deleted after 10 minutes (`outcomeLifetime`); a claimed request with no outcome stays as long as the app that claimed it runs (an export can take an hour).
    - **Processes are told apart by when they started** (`AgentProcess`: the process ID and its start time, from `proc_pidinfo`), so a later process given the same ID isn't taken for it, and a killed one counts as gone from the moment it starts to exit (`sysctl`'s `P_WEXIT`, which comes before `proc_pidinfo` stops reading it, 851-4041), through waiting for its parent as a zombie.
      - **A command that's killed** before an app claims its request: the request is deleted, not run, since nobody waits for it. A command that stops waiting on purpose (it timed out, or Ctrl-C before the claim) calls `cancel(id:)`, which withdraws a request no app has claimed; once claimed, it runs, and the outcome is still written.
      - **The app quitting** after it claimed a request: `outcome(id:)` says it failed (`appQuit`, "lil health quit before it finished.") as soon as the app is gone, and the next claim writes that, so a relaunch doesn't run it again.
    - **Signing out** (`reset`) deletes outcomes with the log, and fails what's unfinished as signed out ("Sign in to lil health first."), so a command waiting on one hears why rather than nothing. Those go 10 minutes later, like other outcomes; they hold only the action, never Health data.
    - **Tested** with separate connections standing for processes (concurrent requests and claims run each request once), `/usr/bin/sqlite3` writing and reading rows as another process, and spawned processes killed to stand for a command or the app.
  - **`lil-health open`, `sync` and `export`** (851-2896), also as `lil health open …`. Not MCP tools: `lil-health mcp` stays read-only for Health data, with the remote server's twenty-five Health tools and the card tools (851-3216, 851-4014), whose writes it asks the app for as action requests, as these commands do (`cards.md`, MCP tools) (the toolkit is defined once and served twice, and the remote server can't open a page on someone's Mac). Making actions tools would be a decision of its own.
    - **`open <page>`:** Summary, a category's name, or a type with a category as the queries take one (`HealthCatalog.type(named:)`: name, identifier or short identifier), in any case, unquoted words joined (`open heart rate`). Exact names only, categories before types (Sleep is the category), as the Open intent's exact matches. Health Details and the characteristics aren't pages (851-3187). An unknown page exits 65 without asking the app or logging.
    - **`sync`** is the action now, and `sync status` stays the query (it had been `sync`'s default). **`export [--to <folder>]`** defaults to Downloads, as the intent does; the folder is sent as an absolute path (relative to the current directory, `~` expanded). A folder that isn't there exits 65 before asking, since retrying won't help; the app checks it again, and whether it can write there.
    - **How each one asks:** `requestAction` with the parent process (77 while access is off, logged as the intents' command text), then it posts the signal if lil health is running, or launches it (`NSWorkspace.openApplication`, in the background except for `open`, which also brings a running app forward, since macOS may keep the terminal in front). The app is the running one, else the one the tool is inside (`Contents/MacOS/lil-health`), else the one Launch Services knows as `tech.lil.health`, as `lil` finds it; through `lil`, these are the same app. A launched app gets the command's `CFFIXED_USER_HOME` and `LIL_API_URL`, so a test's app reads the folder the request is in and talks to its server (release builds ignore `LIL_API_URL`).
    - **How long each one waits:** up to 15 s for the app to claim the request, else it's withdrawn (`cancel`) and the command exits 69 with "lil health didn't answer. Is it installed and signed in?" Then `open` waits for the app's answer, at most 15 s more: not done at the claim, as the ticket had it, since an app launched signed out claims the request and fails it only after its 10 s wait for sign-in, which the command would otherwise report as opened. `sync` waits for the app's answer, which comes within its 60 s, and stops at 75 s with the still-syncing answer if the app has stopped answering (`open` likewise answers opened at its 15 s: the app has the request). `export` waits until the archive is saved, looking once a second rather than five times, then prints its path alone (for `$(lil health export)`). Ctrl-C before the claim withdraws the request ("Stopped. lil health won't do it."); after it, the app goes on (an export is still saved to the folder), and the command says so. Then it ends by SIGINT, as shells expect, so a loop running it stops too.
    - **Output:** text by default (`Opened Steps in lil health.`, `lil health is up to date, as of 2026-10-21 10:41.`, the path); `--json` prints `{"outcome": "ok", "result": …}`, with `{"page", "name"}`, `{"status": "upToDate", "syncedAt"}` (the toolkit's local time with offset) or `{"status": "stillSyncing"}`, and `{"path"}`. Failures print their message on standard error, as the queries' do, with `--json` too.
    - **Exit statuses** (`sysexits.h`, as the queries'): 64 usage, 65 an unknown page or a missing folder, 69 not installed, no answer, signed out, or the app quit before it was done (`appQuit`), 75 (`EX_TEMPFAIL`) the sync or export failed (the app's message, e.g. "Couldn't sync: this Mac is offline."; the app's failures carry no finer reason, so an unwritable folder or an app too old for the request is 75 too), 77 access off (also when it was turned off before the claim), and Ctrl-C ends it by SIGINT (130 in shells).
    - **Tested** with `HealthCLI.run`, a temporary `AgentAccess.sqlite` and a fake app (`HealthCLI.AppConnection`: running or not, installed or not, and a thread that claims and answers the request as the app does), for each command and exit status.
